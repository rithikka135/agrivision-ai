Disease: Pepper Bell Bacterial Spot
Cause: Xanthomonas euvesicatoria / Xanthomonas campestris bacteria.
High-Risk Weather: Warm temperatures (24°C to 30°C), high relative humidity (>85%), and rain or overhead watering.
Chemical Treatment: Apply fixed copper fungicides mixed with Mancozeb or Actigard every 7 to 10 days during humid weather.
Organic Treatment: Spray bio-fungicides containing Bacillus subtilis or use dilute copper soap solutions early in disease onset.
Prevention: Use certified disease-free seeds, avoid overhead irrigation, and rotate crops away from Solanaceous plants for 3 years.

Disease: Potato Early Blight
Cause: Alternaria solani fungus.
High-Risk Weather: Moderate temperatures (20°C to 25°C) with alternating wet and dry conditions.
Chemical Treatment: Apply Chlorothalonil, Mancozeb, or Azoxystrobin at the first sign of leaf spots.
Organic Treatment: Spray copper-based fungicides or bio-fungicides containing Bacillus amyloliquefaciens.
Prevention: Destroy infected crop debris after harvest, rotate crops, and maintain adequate nitrogen fertility.

Disease: Potato Late Blight
Cause: Phytophthora infestans (fungal-like oomycete pathogen).
High-Risk Weather: High relative humidity (>80%) and cool-to-moderate temperatures (15°C to 24°C).
Chemical Treatment: Apply systemic fungicides like Metalaxyl, Cymoxanil, or Chlorothalonil preventatively.
Organic Treatment: Apply copper hydroxide or copper sulfate sprays strictly before prolonged rainy periods.
Prevention: Plant resistant seed tubers, eliminate volunteer potato plants, and ensure proper soil drainage.

Disease: Tomato Bacterial Spot
Cause: Xanthomonas vesicatoria bacteria.
High-Risk Weather: High humidity, rainy conditions, and warm temperatures (24°C to 30°C).
Chemical Treatment: Apply copper hydroxide combined with Mancozeb to reduce bacterial resistance.
Organic Treatment: Apply copper octanoate (copper soap) or bio-bactericides early in the morning.
Prevention: Avoid working in wet fields, prune lower branches to stop soil splash, and sanitize tools.

Disease: Tomato Early Blight
Cause: Alternaria linariae / Alternaria solani fungus.
High-Risk Weather: Warm temperatures with frequent heavy dew or rainfall.
Chemical Treatment: Apply Mancozeb, Chlorothalonil, or Difenoconazole every 7 to 14 days.
Organic Treatment: Apply copper fungicides or potassium bicarbonate solution.
Prevention: Mulch around the base of plants to prevent soil-to-leaf splashing and prune bottom foliage.

Disease: Tomato Late Blight
Cause: Phytophthora infestans.
High-Risk Weather: Cool, wet weather with high humidity (>90%).
Chemical Treatment: Spray Chlorothalonil, Dimethomorph, or Fluazinam immediately upon local outbreak warnings.
Organic Treatment: Apply copper soap formulations every 5 to 7 days during high humidity.
Prevention: Ensure good air movement between rows and destroy all infected foliage immediately.

Disease: Tomato Leaf Mold
Cause: Passalora fulva (Cladosporium fulvum) fungus.
High-Risk Weather: High relative humidity (>85%) and moderate temperatures inside greenhouses or enclosed fields.
Chemical Treatment: Apply Difenoconazole, Chlorothalonil, or Copper Oxychloride.
Organic Treatment: Spray bio-fungicides based on Bacillus subtilis or Trichoderma species.
Prevention: Maximize greenhouse ventilation, maintain wider spacing, and avoid leaf wetness.

Disease: Tomato Septoria Leaf Spot
Cause: Septoria lycopersici fungus.
High-Risk Weather: Extended wet weather with temperatures around 20°C to 25°C.
Chemical Treatment: Apply Chlorothalonil, Mancozeb, or Copper Octanoate sprays.
Organic Treatment: Apply liquid copper sprays or sulfur formulations at early spot detection.
Prevention: Stake plants upright, eliminate solanaceous weeds, and drip irrigate instead of overhead spraying.

Disease: Tomato Spider Mites Two Spotted Spider Mite
Cause: Tetranychus urticae (tiny pest arachnids).
High-Risk Weather: Hot, dry, and dusty environmental conditions.
Chemical Treatment: Apply selective miticides like Abamectin, Bifenazate, or Spiromesifen.
Organic Treatment: Spray insecticidal soap, neem oil, or horticultural oils directly on the undersides of leaves.
Prevention: Keep field borders dust-free, mist foliage periodically during droughts, and encourage predatory mites.

Disease: Tomato Target Spot
Cause: Corynespora cassiicola fungus.
High-Risk Weather: Warm temperatures (25°C to 30°C) with high humidity and rain.
Chemical Treatment: Spray Azoxystrobin, Pyraclostrobin, or Chlorothalonil.
Organic Treatment: Apply copper-based bio-fungicides or bio-control agents like Streptomyces sp.
Prevention: Remove diseased plant residue after harvest and maintain adequate crop spacing.

Disease: Tomato Yellow Leaf Curl Virus
Cause: Begomovirus transmitted by Whiteflies (Bemisia tabaci).
High-Risk Weather: Hot, dry seasons when whitefly populations peak.
Chemical Treatment: Direct chemical control unavailable for viruses; apply Imidacloprid or Acetamiprid against whitefly vectors.
Organic Treatment: Spray neem oil, yellow sticky traps, or insecticidal soaps to suppress whitefly vectors.
Prevention: Use insect mesh screens, reflective silver mulches, and plant TYLCV-resistant hybrids.

Disease: Tomato Mosaic Virus
Cause: Tobamovirus (mechanically transmitted through contact, seed, or tools).
High-Risk Weather: Spreads rapidly in warm, high-density cultivation environments.
Chemical Treatment: No chemical cure exists for viral plant infections.
Organic Treatment: Remove and destroy infected plants immediately to stop field spread.
Prevention: Wash hands with milk/soap before handling plants, disinfect gardening tools, and use certified seeds.
